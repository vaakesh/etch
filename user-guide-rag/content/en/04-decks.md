# Etch: create a deck and understand field limits
<!-- chunk_id: etch.decks.create -->

In “Library” → “Decks”, select “New deck”. Enter a title, optionally add a description, choose the privacy setting, and select “Create”.

A title must contain 3–50 characters and no line breaks. The optional description can contain up to 3000 characters. Leading and trailing whitespace is removed. Add cards before practicing with a new, empty deck.

A deck can hold at most 5000 cards. Deck capacity and practice session size are separate limits: a session can contain at most 500 cards.

# Etch: edit or publish your deck
<!-- chunk_id: etch.decks.edit -->

Open your deck and select “Edit deck”, or use its edit action in the library. Change the title, description, or access setting, then select “Save changes”.

A private deck is available to its owner. A public deck is available to other signed-in users, appears in the catalog, and can be saved for practice. Other users do not gain permission to edit your deck.

Making a public deck private ends its previous public access. Users for whom Etch creates an archive may retain an archived snapshot of its earlier contents. Changing privacy does not revoke personal copies that have already been restored.

# Etch: view the cards in a deck
<!-- chunk_id: etch.decks.view -->

Select a deck title in the library, catalog, or author's profile. The deck page shows its title, author, description, access level, card count, and contents.

Large decks have multiple pages of cards. Use page numbers and the previous or next page controls. A card missing from the first page may be on another page of the deck.

Management actions are available for your own deck. When viewing someone else's deck, missing edit buttons are expected: only the owner can change its contents.

# Etch: delete your own deck
<!-- chunk_id: etch.decks.delete -->

Choose “Delete deck” for your deck in the library or on its page, then confirm “Delete”. The deck disappears from the regular library. “Cancel” closes the dialog without deleting it.

Deleting a deck and permanently deleting an individual card are separate operations. Deleting a deck does not itself instruct Etch to delete all its cards from your card library.

There is no direct undo for deleting the original deck. If Etch created an archive entry available to you, “Deck archive” lets you restore a separate private copy before it expires. Check whether that entry exists; restoration of the original deck at its old link is not guaranteed.
